## H10b

> With the relative residual, the Gauss–Newton curvature of the trained FNO converges as
> s grows (its top Ritz values change little from s = 32 to 128), unlike the PDE's
> discrete operator, whose condition number grows like s² (uniform finite differences)
> or n⁴ (Chebyshev collocation). Its spectrum is expected to decay with the solution
> data's POD spectrum rather than with the Laplacian's.

**Confirmed for the curvature's convergence; supported, approximately, for its decay.**

- **The curvature does not grow with s.** From s = 32 to 128, θ_max changes by +1.6 %
  (Adam run, 34.19 → 34.74), +0.5 % (GN200-D, 4,104 → 4,124) and −0.05 % (T4's GN-D,
  2,089 → 2,088), and θ_10 / θ_max by at most 10 %. Over the same s, κ of the uniform
  finite-difference Laplacian grows 16.8× (389 → 6,536), as s² predicts (16×). The
  Chebyshev κ was not measured (`poisson_conditioning` ran without `datagen` on the VM);
  its n⁴ growth is the design's statement, not a measurement here. The adjoint errors
  (≤ 5.8e-5) show the finite-difference products behind these values are sound.
- **The tangent kernel follows the sine modes and decays far faster than the inverse
  Laplacian.** The log correlation of the kernel diagonal with 1/μ_kl is 0.91–0.95 on
  every run, so the kernel orders the modes as the Laplacian does, but steeply: fitted
  cell by cell, the diagonal falls like (1/μ_kl)^p with p ≈ 2.1–3.4 for the Adam run
  (2.3–2.7 at the higher modes), not p = 1.
- **Its decay is of the order of u's POD spectrum.** Sorted by rank and relative to the
  first, against Phase 6's empirical POD eigenvalues of u
  (`docs/poisson2d/phase6/results.md`, "Leading eigenvalues"; the learning grid at
  s = 64 gives the same ratios to four digits):

  | rank | 1/μ (sorted) | kernel, Adam run | kernel, GN200-D | POD of u |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2 | 4.0e-1 | 1.3e-1 | 1.2e-2 | 7.1e-2 |
  | 4 | 2.5e-1 | 3.7e-2 | 1.0e-2 | 1.6e-2 |
  | 7 | 1.5e-1 | 1.7e-2 | 2.5e-3 | 2.9e-3 |
  | 12 | 1.0e-1 | 5.1e-3 | 1.3e-3 | 6.3e-4 |

  By rank 12 the inverse Laplacian has fallen 10×, the kernels 200–800×, and u's POD
  1,600×. The kernels sit within a factor of about 8 (1.2–8) of the POD spectrum,
  either side, and far below 1/μ_kl. This is one sample's diagonal, and rank-sorting mixes degenerate groups, so it
  is an order-of-magnitude agreement, not a fit.
- **Gauss–Newton finds sharper minima.** The GN-trained FNO's θ_max is 119× the
  Adam-trained one's at s = 64 (4,117 against 34.5), with a wider spread (θ_10 / θ_max
  1.6e-3 against 9.2e-3): second-order training ends in a region of much higher
  curvature, as reported for other second-order methods (SASSHA, 2025). The GN run
  measured here is the matrix's GN200-D at μ = 1e-4, whose error 4 is the higher of the
  two (2.16e-2 against 1.10e-2); the μ = 0 runs' curvature was not measured.
