# Phase 10 results

Generated by `poisson_matrix --by-optimizer` from 15 runs at s = 64, p = 0, N = 1000, each evaluated by `predict_poisson` on the same 200 test samples (n = 129 CGL nodes). Arms: Adam; GN<batch>, Gauss–Newton at that batch; Hybrid, Adam then Gauss–Newton; `-D` the Hutchinson-diagonal preconditioner. Error 3 is the coarse error on the learning grid, error 4 the fine-grid error on the CGL grid (design §13.3); the gap is the final test L² minus the final train L². Updates are optimisation steps taken; passes are forward and backward passes (`costs.json`). Times are wall-clock seconds.

## Provenance

Every Phase 10 run, the matrix above and the follow-ups below, was trained on the VM (one
A100, cuda, f32) at commit `9dbeb91`, on the Burn fork `ax1s-x1zz/burn@ddfa9af`, before
the move to Burn 0.23.0-pre.2 (#119). Later runs on 0.23 are not bit-comparable with
these: compare them only with reruns on the same Burn.

## Cost per update

From `costs.json`. A pass is one forward or one backward pass of the FNO on one batch.

| arm | forward passes per update | backward passes per update | passes per second | sample-passes per second |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adam (batch 20) | 1 | 1 | 58.2 | 1,164 |
| GN20 | 148.7 | 49.1 | 59.2 | 1,183 |
| GN20-D | 153.1 | 51.7 | 59.7 | 1,193 |
| GN200-D | 141.0 | 47.1 | 17.3 | 3,459 |
| Hybrid-D | – (Adam and GN mixed) | – | 58.0 | 1,160 |

At batch 20, Gauss–Newton and Adam run passes at the same rate: the A100 is
overhead-bound there (Phase 5 T5, Phase 9 T3), and GN adds no measurable overhead of its
own. GN's wall-clock is therefore its pass count. One GN update costs about 150 forward
and 50 backward passes, about 100 Adam updates' worth, not the 15–20 that H10a assumed:
CG often runs to its cap of 50 (on about half of T4's steps), and each CG iteration costs about 3 forward
and 1 backward passes (the finite-difference JVP is two forwards, the VJP a forward and a
backward). At batch 200 a pass covers 10× the samples at 3.4× the time, so GN200-D moves
about 2.9× more samples per second than batch 20; that is why it is the best GN arm of the
matrix.

Reading the table:

- **Weight decay is what limits Gauss–Newton.** At μ = 1e-4 no GN run gets below error 4
  2.0e-2, whatever the budget, batch or CG cap. At μ = 0 the same settings reach
  5.6e-3 (CG cap 50) and 3.0e-3 (CG cap 150) in 5,400 s. In Φ, μ is a Tikhonov term whose
  minimiser the step solves for, so GN converges to the penalised minimum; Adam adds the
  same μθ to its gradient and then rescales it per parameter, so the penalty acts more
  weakly (Loshchilov & Hutter 2019).
- **Weight decay helps Adam.** Without it Adam reaches a lower training loss (2.4e-3
  against 3.2e-3) and a higher error 4 (1.539e-2 against 1.061e-2): it overfits, with a
  gap of 1.3e-2. Adam's best configuration is the matrix's, μ = 1e-4.
- **At equal time, GN at μ = 0 against Adam at μ = 1e-4 is not settled by three seeds.**
  The mean is lower (9.06e-3 against 1.061e-2), seeds 43 and 44 lie below Adam's range
  (1.041e-2–1.095e-2), and seed 42 lies above it (1.200e-2). GN's spread across seeds is
  about 10× Adam's.
- **Given 6.3× the time, GN reaches errors no Adam run reached.** CG 150 at μ = 0:
  error 4 3.03e-3, 3.4× below Adam's best seed, and a train L² of 2.37e-3, below Adam's
  3.19e-3: the only GN run to reach the baseline's training loss, in 230 updates against
  25,000, at 94,074 passes against 50,000 and 5,409 s against 859 s. One seed only, and
  no Adam run was given 5,400 s; Adam's gap of 7.4e-3 at 500 epochs suggests more epochs
  would not lower its error 4, but that is not measured.
- **GN generalises: its gap stays at about 2.5e-3 or below** in the follow-ups (2.6e-4 to
  2.5e-3; up to 5.0e-3 on one matrix run, GN20-D seed 44), against 7.4e-3 for Adam. Error 4 follows GN's training loss.
- **Full batch is worse than batch 200** at equal time (2.12e-2 against 5.58e-3 at μ = 0):
  fewer updates (133 against 475) for about a fifth of the passes, at 4.5× the memory.
- **More CG iterations pay off at μ = 0** (3.03e-3 against 5.58e-3 at the same time) but
  not at μ = 1e-4 (2.45e-2 against 2.01e-2).

## H10a

> Gauss–Newton reaches the baseline's training loss in far fewer updates than Adam, but
> each update costs about 15–20 Adam updates at batch 20. Whether it reaches a lower
> fine-grid error at equal A100 wall-clock is unknown.

**Partly confirmed, with the cost estimate wrong and the equal-time question open.**

- **Fewer updates: yes, but only without weight decay.** At G5's μ = 1e-4 no GN run
  reached Adam's final train L² of 3.19e-3 (best 1.83e-2, GN200-D given 5,400 s). At μ = 0, CG 150
  reached 2.37e-3 in 230 updates against Adam's 25,000. At N = 100 (T4) GN-D matched
  Adam's test L² after 25 updates where Adam needed 695.
- **Cost per update: about 100 Adam updates, not 15–20** (150 forward and 50 backward
  passes; "Cost per update"). Passes run at the same rate for both optimisers, so
  wall-clock follows passes.
- **Lower error 4 at equal wall-clock: no at G5's settings; open at μ = 0.** Every arm of
  the matrix lost to Adam (best GN 2.16e-2 against 1.061e-2). At μ = 0, GN200-D's mean is
  15 % below Adam's, but one seed of three is above Adam's range. More seeds are post-Phase
  10 work.
- **The caveat held the other way round.** H10a warned that a lower training loss need
  not lower error 4. For GN the two move together (gap ≤ 2.5e-3 in the follow-ups, ≤ 5.0e-3 in the matrix); it is Adam, at μ = 0,
  whose lower training loss raises error 4.

H10b is answered in [conditioning.md](conditioning.md).
